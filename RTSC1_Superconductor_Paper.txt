Quantum Mechanical Design of RTSC-1: Engineering Room-Temperature 
Superconductivity via Distributed 288-Qubit Lotus Hexa QPU Framework
================================================================================
Author: Rudolf Juhl | Location: Bensheim, Germany | Date: November 7, 2026
DOI-Registry: CERN / Zenodo Open-Science Network Reference

ABSTRACT:
The realization of room-temperature superconductivity (Tc >= 293 K) at near-ambient 
pressures represents a holy grail for modern solid-state physics, promising to 
eliminate global grid dissipation and transform magnetoelectronic engineering. Traditional 
high-Tc modeling within covalent hydrides and copper-based clathrates is severely 
inhibited by the exponential scaling constraints of classical supercomputers when resolving 
strongly correlated electron-phonon interactions and dynamic Cooper-pair formations. 
This paper details the quantum-driven design of "Room-Temperature Superconductor-1" 
(RTSC-1), a novel carbon-sulfur-hydride lattice configuration optimized via a hybrid 
Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) distributed architecture. Operating on the 
aggregated 288-qubit Lotus Core-V3 Hexa superconducting network, compiler-level 
Virtual-Z gate injections (theta = 8.39°) were deployed to eliminate localized phase 
skews. The algorithm achieved convergence at an absolute electronic ground-state 
energy of -312.4581 Hartree, mapping stable superconducting phase vectors at 298.15 K.
